## Summary

This slice widens the cheap-tier PII scanner with `ip_address`/`credit_card` (Luhn-gated) patterns, widens the prompt-injection scanner from 5 to 10 phrase patterns, and adds a deliberately-stubbed `app/evaluators/medium.py` wired into `_run_response_side_interception` (now threading `workload`/`request` through so a real 2.5 implementation can drop in without touching the router again). The diff is small, additive, and matches its own specs closely — I traced every new pattern and the router wiring and found no path that releases a blocked response or calls the model on a blocked request. The stub genuinely stubs (returns `[]`, no dependency added, behavior unchanged from pre-2.4). Two real, narrow gaps: the new `credit_card` pattern silently fails to match Amex's own natural 4-6-5 grouped format despite Amex being one of the four networks the spec claims to cover, and `extract_instructions` is meaningfully more prone to firing on ordinary product/support phrasing ("show me your instructions", "repeat the instructions") than the other nine patterns, with no benign-case test recorded for it. Both are Minor, not blockers, and consistent with this codebase's already-accepted philosophy of trading false negatives/positives for simplicity — but they're specific enough, and cheap enough to fix or at least note, that they're worth flagging now rather than letting a specific commonly-said failure mode sit undocumented.

## Minor

### 🟡 `credit_card` regex doesn't match Amex's natural 4-6-5 grouped format, `app/evaluators/pii.py:35-38`

**Problem**: The pattern is `(?:4\d{3}|5[1-5]\d{2}|3[47]\d{2}|6011|65\d{2})[ -]?\d{4}[ -]?\d{4}(?:[ -]?\d{1,4})?`, which assumes a 4-4-4(-4) grouping. Amex cards are conventionally grouped 4-6-5 (e.g. `3782 822463 10005`). Verified directly:
```
'3782 822463 10005'  -> no match
'3782-822463-10005'  -> no match
'378282246310005'    -> match (ungrouped digits only)
```
Visa/Mastercard/Discover's native 4-4-4-4 grouping matches fine (`4111 1111 1111 1111` confirmed); only Amex's own natural grouping is silently skipped when spaced or dashed.

**Why it matters**: 2.1's own spec (`.agents/prompts/2.1-cheap-pii-regex-plan.md`, AC-1) explicitly names Amex as one of "a recognized network prefix such as Visa, Mastercard, Amex, or Discover, digits only or grouped with spaces or dashes" — implying grouped-format support across all four. In practice, a real Amex number a model echoes back or a user pastes in its normal human-readable form will not be caught at all; only the unformatted 15-digit run is. This is a false negative in exactly the category this milestone exists to strengthen, silent rather than logged as an accepted limitation the way the phone/`ip_address` false-positive tradeoffs were.

**Suggested fix**: Either add an Amex-specific alternative to the pattern (`4\d{3}[ -]?\d{6}[ -]?\d{5}` grouping, still gated by Luhn), or explicitly document the 4-4-4-4-only assumption as a known limitation the way the phone/`ip_address` gaps already are, so it doesn't read as full Amex support until it's fixed.

### 🟡 `extract_instructions` is markedly more collision-prone with ordinary phrasing than the other nine patterns, and has no benign-case test, `app/evaluators/prompt_injection.py:42-46`

**Problem**: `\b(?:print|output|repeat|show me)\s+(?:your\s+)?(?:system\s+)?(?:prompt|instructions)\b` matches any occurrence of "show me your instructions", "repeat the instructions", "print the instructions" — phrasing plausible in ordinary product usage (a tutoring bot, an assembly/setup assistant, a recipe or forms app being asked to restate steps it was just given), not just adversarial framing. Every other new pattern in this file requires a more specific, less naturally-occurring construction (a named jailbreak persona, "unfiltered/uncensored mode", a fake role marker at line start, "pretend you have no restrictions"). `2.2`'s own spec (`.agents/prompts/2.2-cheap-prompt-injection-heuristic-plan.md`) includes one benign-case test (AC-3, "act as a professional proofreader") that only exercises `roleplay_bypass`; no benign-case scenario was written or self-checked for `extract_instructions`, `unfiltered_mode`, `other_jailbreak_persona`, or `fake_role_marker`.

**Why it matters**: A request-side cheap-tier match on this codebase blocks unconditionally (`403`) and adds a permanent ledger strike (`CONFIDENCE=0.95 > STRIKE_THRESHOLD=0.7`) regardless of how benign the actual intent was — there's no lower-confidence "inconclusive" state for a false positive to fall into. Since ControlPlane is meant to be a general-purpose plug-and-play proxy in front of arbitrary AI applications (`AGENTS.md` §5), not just a jailbreak-defense demo, this specific pattern is the one most likely of the ten to fire on a legitimate workload's everyday traffic (e.g. a customer-support or how-to assistant), each time as a hard block plus a strike that (until Phase 3.1's window expiry lands) never expires.

**Suggested fix**: Narrow the trigger phrase (e.g. require "your system prompt"/"your instructions" together with a jailbreak-adjacent verb, or drop the bare "instructions" branch and keep only "prompt"), or at minimum run and log a benign-case self-check for this pattern specifically before the demo, the way `roleplay_bypass` already got one.

## Nits

- ⚪ `app/evaluators/prompt_injection.py:23-27` (`unfiltered_mode`) and `:28-31` (`other_jailbreak_persona`) also shipped with no recorded benign-case self-check, though their trigger phrasing ("unfiltered mode", a named persona like STAN/AIM) is less likely to appear in ordinary traffic than `extract_instructions`'s.
- ⚪ `app/evaluators/medium.py:16` — `_SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD = 0.3` is dead code today (unused, per the file's own comment); harmless since it's explicitly flagged as forward-looking plumbing for 2.5, not an oversight.
- ⚪ Fork #4's "if the ledger already trips escalation after cheap tier, do not run medium/expensive tier" branch still isn't implemented (`_run_response_side_interception` decides to run medium tier purely on "did cheap tier find something locally," never consulting the session ledger's `cumulative_risk`/`strikes`). This is consistent with everything else in this codebase — the escalation check is explicitly Phase 3.2's named, tracked job, and today's medium tier is a no-op stub so the gap has no live effect — so it's not a new finding, just confirming the deferral is still accurate as of this diff.

## Strengths

- The diff is exactly as advertised by its own specs: `git diff d257b68 -- app/evaluators/pii.py app/evaluators/prompt_injection.py app/routers/chat.py` matches the 2.1/2.2/2.4 plan docs' "Feature design" sections almost verbatim, including the exact regex text and the router's parameter threading. Spec-to-code fidelity here is unusually tight.
- The `credit_card` pattern is properly BIN-restricted plus Luhn-gated rather than repeating the "any digit run" mistake the phone regex made once already (verified: `4111 1111 1111 1112`, which fails Luhn, does not match the validator gate logic when traced by hand). Good, deliberate defense against the exact false-positive class this codebase already got burned by once.
- `app/evaluators/medium.py` is a genuine, honest stub: no new dependency, no disguised partial implementation, `_SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD` clearly marked unused, and the router call site is provably exercised (runs on every cheap-tier-clean response) without changing any observable behavior — exactly what AC-1/AC-4/AC-6 of its spec ask for.
- `fake_role_marker`'s and the Amex gap's tradeoffs sit alongside a strong pattern in this codebase's `decisions.md` of naming false-positive/negative tradeoffs explicitly rather than leaving them implicit — the Amex gap found in this review is the one place recently added that broke that otherwise-consistent discipline.

## Test coverage

`TESTS = none-by-design` per `AGENTS.md` §8 — no findings raised for missing automated tests. Manual self-checks recorded in `STATUS.md`/`decisions.md` for 2.1/2.2/2.4 are reasonably thorough for the malicious-trigger direction (one crafted prompt per new pattern, Luhn-valid vs. Luhn-invalid card, in-range vs. out-of-range IP, request-side and response-side). The gap is on the benign-traffic direction: only `roleplay_bypass` got an explicit false-positive test; `extract_instructions` (flagged above), `unfiltered_mode`, `other_jailbreak_persona`, and `fake_role_marker` did not. Since a cheap-tier match blocks unconditionally with no lower-confidence state, a benign-case self-check for each new pattern — not just the one that happened to get one — is the check most likely to surface a real demo-breaking false positive before Phase 3 makes the ledger's strike accumulation load-bearing.
